Algorithm & Methodology

The core engine behind this dashboard is a Logistic Regression model trained on Weight of Evidence (WOE) transformed features, a gold standard in traditional credit risk scorecards.

  • Weight of Evidence (WOE) Binning: Continuous and categorical variables were bucketed into discrete bins. Each bin was assigned a WOE value reflecting the log-odds ratio of good vs. bad customers in that bin. This handles non-linear relationships and missing values natively.
  • Linear Predictor: The logistic regression model assigns a strict coefficient to each WOE variable. Because WOE handles the non-linearities, the logistic regression serves as a clean, highly interpretable linear aggregator.
  • Scorecard Transformation: The log-odds predictions are mapped to a human-readable scale. The model is scaled such that a score of 600 represents baseline odds of 50:1, and it takes 20 points to double the odds (PDO) of a good outcome.

Business Objective: High Recall Prioritization

Financial institutions typically prefer to avoid bad loans (defaults) even at the expense of rejecting some marginally good loans. Missing a default (False Negative) is much more expensive than a missed opportunity (False Positive).

To align with this objective, the model's decision threshold was mathematically optimized on the validation set using the F2 Score. The F2 Score weights Recall twice as heavily as Precision.

  • High Recall ({{ "%.1f"|format(metrics.get('Recall', 0) * 100) }}%): The model successfully captures the vast majority of actual defaults.
  • Trade-off: This safety mechanism naturally results in a lower Precision ({{ "%.1f"|format(metrics.get('Precision', 0) * 100) }}%), meaning a higher rate of False Positives (good applicants flagged for manual review or rejection).
